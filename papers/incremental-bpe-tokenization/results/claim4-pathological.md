## What was measured

Input `"a" * n`, dictionary = powers of `a` up to 16 with a doubling
merge chain (the paper's Section 7.2 repeated-character family).
Correctness of the incremental tokenizer was cross-checked against the
from-scratch oracle at every size **before** timing.

- **`incremental`** — our reproduction, fed byte-by-byte then emitting
  all tokens (the streaming use-case).
- **`restart_per_byte`** — the honest quadratic baseline: re-tokenize
  the whole prefix from scratch after every appended byte (what any
  non-incremental tokenizer must do in a streaming setting).
- **`batch_oracle`** — one from-scratch pass over the full string
  (non-streaming reference, for context).

**The paper's baseline (`tiktoken`) could not run here:** its encoding
files download from a CDN this sandbox's egress policy blocks. The
mechanism behind the paper's Figure 3 decay (from-scratch re-merging
over growing prefixes) is what `restart_per_byte` isolates; a direct
tiktoken measurement remains to be done in a less restricted
environment.

## Results

| method | per-byte cost (median) | log-log slope of total vs n | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| `incremental` (ours) | ~1.9 µs/byte, flat from n=1 000 to n=100 000 | **0.995** | linear — stable throughput |
| `restart_per_byte` | 6.8 → 81.5 µs/byte, doubling as n doubles | **1.901** | quadratic — throughput decays |
| `batch_oracle` | ~80 ns/byte, flat | **0.960** | linear (sanity check) |

## Interpretation (qualitative claim only)

The paper's Claim 4 is: incremental tokenization holds stable
throughput on adversarial repeated-character inputs while from-scratch
(tiktoken-style) tokenization decays as O(n²). In our environment:

- our incremental implementation's throughput is **flat across a 100×
  input-size range** (slope 0.995 ≈ 1.0), and
- the from-scratch-per-byte baseline **decays quadratically**
  (slope 1.901 ≈ 2.0), already ~43× slower per byte at n=2 000.

This reproduces the claim's *shape* exactly. Absolute numbers are pure
Python and carry no information about the paper's Rust throughput —
they are reported because the protocol requires it, not for comparison
with Table 1/Figure 3 magnitudes.

## Noise and limitations (protocol §4/§5)

- Shared cloud sandbox: 4-core VM, no CPU pinning, idleness not
  guaranteed. Runs with CV > 5% are visible in the full tables
  (notably `batch_oracle`'s sub-millisecond points, CV up to 25%) —
  dispersion is reported alongside every number.
- No direct tiktoken measurement (blocked egress), as noted above.
- One dictionary family; the wide-shallow-forest caveat from the Phase 2
  notes applies (this family does not stress tree depth — that is
  measured separately in `claim2-depth-scaling`).
