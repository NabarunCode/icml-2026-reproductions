## What was measured, and why

Our reproduction implements the paper's per-level search (O(1)
DFS-interval test + sibling binary search) but **not** Section 5.3's
Centroid Decomposition, which bounds the number of levels visited to
O(log t). Rather than assert what this gap costs, we measured it, on a
maximally deep successor chain: vocab `{a, b, ab, aab, …, a^d b}`,
forest = a single chain of depth d, input `(a^d b)` repeated.

Predictions were written into the benchmark script *before* running:

1. **Amortized per-byte cost stays flat** in d — on this family the
   full-depth walk happens only on each `b` byte (once per d+1 bytes),
   so an O(d) walk amortizes to O(1)/byte. Flatness is *not* evidence
   the gap is closed.
2. **Worst single `feed(byte)` latency grows linearly** in d — the
   un-balanced walk visits O(depth) levels on the `b` byte, where the
   paper's centroid-balanced search predicts polylogarithmic growth.

## Results

| metric | measured | log-log slope vs depth | prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chain_amortized` (per-byte) | ~1.5 µs/byte, flat from d=16 to d=512 | 0.961 for totals (input length ∝ d) ⇒ flat per byte | confirmed |
| `feed_max_latency` (worst single byte) | 15 µs → 402 µs, doubling as d doubles (~0.8 µs per level) | **0.972 ≈ linear in d** | confirmed |

## Interpretation

Both predictions held, which pins down precisely what Centroid
Decomposition buys and what its absence costs in our reproduction:

- **Throughput on chain-completion-sparse inputs is unaffected** — deep
  walks are rare enough to amortize. Our earlier Phase-2/Phase-4 notes
  claimed this qualitatively; it is now measured.
- **Tail latency is where the gap lives**: the worst single-byte cost
  grows linearly with forest depth (slope 0.972), reaching ~0.4 ms per
  byte at depth 512 in Python. The full paper algorithm bounds this at
  O(log²t) per byte — for a streaming tokenizer feeding an LLM, that
  per-byte worst case is exactly the latency guarantee Section 5.3
  exists to provide.
- Reading Appendix J closely for this experiment also clarified that
  the paper's 37 ms-vs-15.3 s adversarial construction targets
  rust-gems' per-backtrack O(t²) *validation* cost, which our search
  does not perform — so that specific trap does not transfer to us;
  depth-driven tail latency is our gap's actual signature.

## Noise and limitations (protocol §4/§5)

- Max-statistics are inherently noisy (CV 11–44% on `feed_max_latency`;
  flagged per protocol §4) — but the systematic doubling across five
  consecutive depth doublings is far outside that noise.
- Same sandbox caveats as the Claim 4 run (4-core shared VM, no
  pinning).
- This measures the *cost of the gap*, not the paper's O(log²t) bound
  itself — verifying the bound requires implementing Centroid
  Decomposition (the planned follow-up), at which point this benchmark
  re-run should show `feed_max_latency` flattening to polylog.
